In December 2025 the Texas Attorney General sued five TV makers, Samsung among them, over automatic content recognition (ACR). A judge signed a temporary restraining order against Samsung on 2026-01-05 and set it aside the next day. On 2026-02-26 the Attorney General announced an agreement: Samsung must halt collection or processing of ACR viewing data without Texas consumers' express consent, and must update its smart TVs with clear consent screens. The announced requirement covers Texas consumers. It is not a worldwide switch-off.

This is a news summary, not legal advice. We read the Attorney General's announcement but not the executed agreement.

## What did Texas allege?

Privacy Guides' March 2026 report quotes the Attorney General as alleging that the technology can "capture screenshots of a user's television display every 500 milliseconds" and that it tracks viewing as it happens and sends it to the company without the user's knowledge or consent. Its December post says the manufacturers take screenshots "as often as every half second" and that the process of turning ACR off is "not always obvious".

These are the state's allegations. The agreement announcement is not a finding of fact about how any TV behaves.

## What happened with the restraining order?

[BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/texas-court-blocks-samsung-from-tracking-tv-viewing-then-vacates-order/amp/) reports that the court signed the order on 2026-01-05 at 10:10 AM. It barred Samsung Electronics America and Samsung Electronics Co., Ltd. from using, selling, collecting and transferring ACR viewing data from Texas TVs until 2026-01-19, and it found "good cause to believe" that Samsung's process for enrolling consumers in ACR collection was false. The judge vacated the order on 2026-01-06, one day later, on the court's own motion. A Samsung representative told BleepingComputer a hearing remained scheduled for 2026-01-09. We did not find a source that says what happened at that hearing.

## What did Samsung agree to?

The [Attorney General's release](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-paxton-secures-major-agreement-samsung-ensure-texans-are-protected-smart-tvs) says the agreement is with Samsung Electronics America, Inc. and that Samsung:

- must halt any collection or processing of ACR viewing data without obtaining Texas consumers' express consent;
- must "promptly update its smart TVs and implement disclosures and consent screens that are clear and conspicuous" so Texans can make an informed decision about whether their data is collected and how it is used.

[Privacy Guides](https://www.privacyguides.org/news/2026/03/02/samsung-forced-to-halt-data-collection-in-tvs-in-texas-without-express-consent/) adds that the consent screens should be "void of dark patterns" and "clearly communicate the data that will be collected".

[Fox 7 Austin](https://www.fox7austin.com/news/samsung-updates-tv-data-consent-settings-after-texas-sues-over-spying) quotes Samsung's statement of 2026-02-26: Samsung "shares the Texas Attorney General's goal of promoting transparent and consumer-friendly privacy practices" and "Samsung TVs do not spy on consumers". It also quotes Samsung as saying it maintains that its original television privacy policy and notices followed existing Texas state regulations.

## Who does the Samsung ACR agreement cover?

The announced consent requirements cover Texas consumers. [Malwarebytes](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/news/2026/03/samsung-tvs-stop-spying-on-viewers-in-texas-heres-how-to-disable-acr-anywhere) writes that the settlement changes apply to Texas customers and that users elsewhere can adjust the settings manually.

The requirement is consent before collection. An owner who agrees to the prompt can still have ACR on. The agreement is also separate from technical tests of what happens after opt-out; one such study, from October 2026, is covered in [how to turn off ACR](/privacy/turn-off-acr-samsung-tv/). The release does not say when the new screens reach TVs, how consent will be verified or what the wording will be, and we have not seen the agreement text.

## Are there other cases against Samsung?

[Malwarebytes (2026-10-27)](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/news/2026/10/samsung-tvs-without-mics-may-listen-through-soundbars-researcher-says) reports a New York class action filed in January and voluntarily dismissed in July, and a California class action filed in October that accuses Samsung Electronics America of not being open about its ACR data collection. These are allegations. We have not read the filings.

As of the 2026-02-26 announcement, the Attorney General said his suits against Sony, LG, and Hisense and TCL Technology remained ongoing. We have not checked their current status.
