Firmware updates

Can you downgrade Samsung TV firmware? What reports say

Updated 10 November 2026 · Checked on 2026-11-10 against Samsung's developer FAQ, a Samsung US community thread, two SamyGO forum threads, and the Vetro README and CHANGELOG for release 0.5.0.

No, not officially. Samsung's developer FAQ answers the question directly: "For security reasons, downgrading the TV firmware is not supported." Third-party reports on the SamyGO forum say a downgrade was possible on some older models with root. We give no instructions, because those reports are old and model-specific, and a bad image can leave a TV unusable. Vetro does not offer downgrades.

What does Samsung say about downgrading?#

Samsung's developer FAQ on device information and firmware says downgrading the TV firmware is not supported, and that forcing firmware updates is not supported either.

Samsung's consumer community shows what that looks like in practice. In a US community thread from August 2023, an owner wrote that a TV had auto-updated from v14 to v15, after which the Home menu, Apps and Sources disappeared and the smart features stopped working. They had tried a power drain, a Smart Hub reset and a factory reset over three days. The answer marked as accepted, from a Samsung community manager, gave no downgrade steps and suggested calling Samsung and asking for its remote management team. That is one thread, not a policy statement.

What do SamyGO posters report?#

SamyGO is a community forum for Samsung TV modding. Both threads below are old, and neither proves anything about current TVs.

In a K-series thread in SamyGO's Tizen TV forum, an owner of a UN70KU6300 asked how to downgrade after the TV did not detect firmware copied from Samsung's site to a thumb drive, and whether the service menu could help. A moderator replied that downgrading is not possible. In April 2018 a poster suggested raising the version number of an older file so the TV treats it as an upgrade, noting that the file is probably encrypted; the thread presents this as a theory. Another poster said a downgrade is possible with root, with the same procedure as for the J series, and that without root there is no other way. The thread contains no confirmed procedure.

In an F-series thread from February to March 2018, an owner of an already-rooted F6350 reported going from firmware 2225 to 1116 with root still working, then back to 1200. The instructions were in a donor-only section of the forum, so the page contains no procedure either.

Taken together, these posts suggest the exceptions rely on root and on specific, older models. We don't know that anything in them applies to Tizen 6 or newer.

Can root help on a current TV?#

We can't say. The samsung-tv-root README that Vetro builds on records results for exact models and firmware and doesn't mention downgrades. See Vetro's root requirements and whether rooting a Samsung TV is safe.

Vetro offers prevention, not a rollback: a Quick settings switch, Disable automatic firmware update, so that a new firmware does not remove root behind your back (README). See stopping Samsung TV auto updates. If an update has already happened, will a firmware update remove root lists what Vetro's records show. Vetro is experimental, needs root and ships no signed installer; see how to install Vetro with Apps2Samsung.

Why do people ask for a downgrade?#

The threads give the reasons. After an update, one owner lost the Home menu, Apps and Sources. A SamyGO poster's TV began refusing playback from a USB drive after an update and he asked how to go back. If your problem is a lost feature, ask Samsung support first and check whether a later update fixes it.

What would a downgrade risk on a rooted TV?#

Nothing we can quantify. The risks are the plain ones: an image the TV rejects, a TV that doesn't boot, and loss of root. The README says a firmware update can change the rooting result, and we wouldn't touch firmware without a way to recover, which none of these sources provides.

How can I avoid needing a downgrade?#

You can't avoid every case, but you can cut the unplanned ones. Turn automatic updates off (how), note your exact firmware before you accept a manual update, and check the README compatibility table for your model. Vetro's diagnostic report records the model and firmware.

What if an update broke something?#

Start with Samsung support, as the community thread above suggests, and Samsung's own support pages. If Vetro stopped working after an update, open an issue with a diagnostic report: the README asks for the exact model and firmware, and failed attempts help too. See uninstalling Vetro if you want to go back to a stock TV.

Limits#

The SamyGO pages are fan-run forum threads about older model families, quoted for what people reported. Samsung's community thread is one answer on one day. Check Samsung's terms for warranty questions.
