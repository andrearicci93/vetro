# Vetro UI

Vetro UI is everything Vetro draws on the TV, gathered on one page of the dashboard: the **launcher**, **live TV** (Vetro TV Tuner) and the **on-screen displays** (the volume bar and the number pad). It replaces the separate Launcher and Vetro TV Tuner pages.

The page has four tabs:

| Tab | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| **Overview** | One switch per part, its state, what it still needs, and how the parts depend on each other |
| **Launcher** | What the Home key opens: the Vetro launcher (with its preview, the task manager, HDMI and TizenTube options) or **another app** you installed (the custom launcher). Samsung’s Home switch lives here too |
| **Live TV** | The tuner: its switch, each of its parts (banner, channel list, TV guide (EPG), number pad, info panel, audio key, hold-to-repeat), the remote buttons and a picture of every screen |
| **On-screen displays** | The volume bar (with its position) and the number pad, what they need, and a picture of each that follows the chosen position |

Old links still work: opening the former Launcher or Tuner page opens Vetro UI on the matching tab.

## What depends on what

These rules are in `app/service/vetro-ui.js`, shared by the service and the page, and covered by `app/tests/vetro-ui.test.cjs`.

- **One launcher on the Home key.** Samsung’s Home, the Vetro launcher or another app: never two of Vetro’s at once. When one is on, the other offers a single **Use … instead** button: one question, then Vetro turns the first off and the second on, with the same steps as their own switches. If the second step fails, Samsung’s Home answers the Home key until you try again.
- **The volume bar and the 123 pad are drawn by the launcher’s process.** It runs while the Vetro launcher is on and, in the background, while another app is the launcher (in that mode the launcher’s process draws nothing of its own but still answers these keys; not yet tried on a TV). With Samsung’s Home on the Home key the two switches are kept but do nothing, and the page says so.
- **They need the remote interception.** The Remapper’s input bridge holds Vol +, Vol −, Mute and 123 back from Samsung and hands them to the launcher. **Vetro UI turns the interception on by itself** when you switch one of them on (or when the launcher that draws them comes on): it picks the one remote that reports keys, as the Remapper’s restore does. With more than one remote it does not guess and the page says to choose it in the Remapper. When both parts go off again, an interception Vetro UI turned on itself goes off too, unless shortcuts of your own still need it. It never turns the interception on at start-up or in the background: if you switch it off in the Remapper, it stays off.
- **The held keys are listed in the Remapper**, first among the shortcuts, with a lock: they cannot be deleted there, only switched off in Vetro UI. They are worked out from the launcher’s switches each time (`GET /remap` returns them as `reserved`) and never written into your rules, so an older Vetro or a restore never finds stray rules.
- **Vetro UI’s keys come first.** A shortcut of yours on Vol, Mute or 123 does not run while the part that uses that key is on; the Remapper marks it *Overridden by Vetro UI*. Before a part takes a key you use, Vetro UI names the shortcut and asks; saving a new shortcut on a held key asks too, and so does switching the interception off while Vetro UI needs it.
- **A launcher coming on can wake parts that were waiting.** With Samsung’s Home on the Home key, a volume bar or pad that is switched on holds no key. Turning on the Vetro launcher or another app makes them work, so the question before it names any shortcut of yours that would stop running.
- **Shortcuts on the tuner’s keys.** While the remote interception is on, a key that one of your shortcuts uses goes to that shortcut and is never passed on, so the tuner never gets it (CH ±, OK, the arrows, Info, Guide, Back). The Live TV tab lists such shortcuts, the Remapper marks them *Live TV does not get …*, and saving a new one on a tuner key asks first. The shortcut wins here: giving the tuner precedence would need the bridge to stop holding those keys while the tuner is on screen, which is not built. A shortcut whose action **sends** one of those keys does reach the tuner while it is on screen (on its control pipe); sent by name it would go into the tuner’s window and be lost.
- **The row is Samsung's, then yours.** The launcher's apps row starts as Samsung's Home row. On the TV, hold OK (0.8 s) on a tile to move it: Left / Right carry it, Down takes it out of the row, OK keeps it there, Back puts the row back as it was. **All apps**, the pill at the end of the row (where Settings was), lists every app Samsung's own Apps screen lists, by name, plus Samsung TV Plus and Live TV, with Settings and the App store as its last two pills; a tick marks the apps already in the row, and holding OK on an app adds it to the end of the row or takes it out. The launcher sends the row to the service, which keeps it in the launcher's state (the order, the tiles taken out) and lays it over Samsung's row at every start: an app you install later still arrives at the end, an app you uninstall leaves quietly, and Samsung's own Home row is never changed. A tap on OK now opens a tile when the key comes up, not when it goes down. The Launcher tab's **Use Samsung's row again** forgets your row. Not tried on a TV yet: holding OK on a physical remote, and the row reaching the service.
- **The Control center is part of the Vetro launcher.** Down on the apps row opens up to three cards side by side; Left and Right move between them, Up (on a card's first row) or Back returns. Every card is off until you switch it on (Launcher tab: one switch per card; Overview: one for the whole, which turns on the Task manager). **Task manager**: the running apps with their memory, biggest first; OK switches to one, holding OK (0.8 s) closes it. By zabique (PR #172). **Audio output**: the TV speakers and the paired Bluetooth audio devices, the one that plays ticked; OK sends the sound there with the call Samsung's own Sound output list makes, and a device that is not connected yet is connected first (not tried on a TV yet). One card is as wide as the Task manager always was; two or three are narrower and the Task manager then leaves out the CPU column. When a card is on, a hint sits under the bar (the card's name, or “Control center” for more than one), which moves 46 px higher to make room. It needs no interception, and with another app as the launcher it does nothing (nothing of the dock is drawn then). It reads Down, Left, Right and OK, so a shortcut of yours on one of them stops it from working while the interception is on; the Launcher tab says so. An older setting with only the Task manager switch reads as that card alone.
- **Live TV stands alone.** It needs no launcher and no interception: while the bridge holds the remote, the tuner reads the keys from the bridge’s forwarded copy of the remote (same name). Each time the bridge starts it makes a new copy, and the service tells the launcher and the tuner to pick it up at once (before, they found it within three seconds and heard nothing meanwhile). The 123 pad’s Done tunes the tuner when live TV is on screen, and its Info key opens the tuner’s info panel.
- **One window answers each key.** The launcher and the tuner read the remote straight from the input device, so they see every key whatever window gets it; each of them now answers only the keys that are its own. While the osd 123 is open, the Dock and the Task manager under it do not move (before, Right moved both and OK on Done also opened the focused tile). While the Dock, the Task manager or the osd 123 is open, the tuner does not answer or wake up (CH+ with the Dock open used to bring the tuner up over it). When Samsung’s own 123 pad opens (Vetro’s is off, or the key came on a part of the remote the bridge does not hold), the Dock steps aside and the tuner closes its panels. The tuner leaves the keys alone while another window has the focus, never shows itself over another app, covers the TV input only (never HDMI), and comes back when the launcher closes without opening anything. The osd 123 sends its keys once its window is gone, never into itself, and its colour and teletext keys first send the tuner away so they reach Samsung’s live TV app. [Details](TUNER.md#what-replaces-samsungs-tuner-means).

## Compatibility

Nothing is moved or renamed on the TV: the launcher’s state (`vetro-dock.json`), the tuner’s state, the routes `/dock` and `/tuner` and the plugin ids `launcher` and `tuner` are the same. Whoever had the launcher or the tuner on before an update finds them on, with the same options. A launcher state from an older version has no position for the volume bar: it reads as Left, the default, and has the task manager off, so the launcher looks and works as before. The remote setting gains one optional field, `auto`, which older versions ignore.

## Not tried on a TV

The page, the switch-over and the automatic interception are tested on the computer only. Not yet seen on a TV: the bar and pad in custom-launcher mode, the automatic choice of the remote, the tuner reading the forwarded remote right after the bridge starts, and the key ownership rules of 2026-11-12 (focus, the launcher’s flags, `resume`, HDMI, the front app). The Task manager was tried by its author on a QN85A (opening, sorting, Back) before it became a Control center card; the Control center itself (two or three cards, Left / Right, the Audio output card), tapping and holding OK on a physical remote, and any run on a Q80A are still to do.
