# Diagnostic report export

The Diagnostics panel downloads `vetro-diagnostics.json` through GET `/diagnostics/download`. The report stays on the user's computer. Vetro does not upload it or record watched content.

The `vetro-diagnostics` format has schema version 1. `appVersion` and `sourceRevision` identify the installed package; an unbuilt development checkout may have a null revision. Each of the 17 sections contains `status` (`ok`, `unavailable`, or `timeout`) and, when available, `data`. A section that is not `ok` also carries `reason`, one of `no-root-agent`, `command-timeout`, `command-failed`, `incomplete`, `unreadable`, `report-deadline`, `no-reader`, `not-requested` or `error`, plus the numeric `exitCode` of a failed command. Reasons come from a fixed list; command output and error text are never exported. `sourceDirty` is true when files under `app/` or `rooting/` differed from `sourceRevision` when the package was built, false when they matched, and null when unknown. Readers only inspect existing state; no flags, processes, settings, or services are changed. Reads have a 20-second report deadline and concurrent downloads share the same collection. The monitor section takes two samples about 1.5 seconds apart so CPU and fault rates can be computed; if the second sample fails or the deadline is near, the first is exported without rates. A timed-out platform read may still finish after the report returns.

Component details (`components`, off by default): the catalog behind the Debloater comes from one Q80A image, so reports from other TVs list many packages Vetro cannot describe (`com.samsung.tv.iacr` in two reports). When the Diagnostics switch *Include details about unrecognised components* is on (`GET /diagnostics/download?components=1`), the service reads those packages' manifests (`/opt/share/packages/<package>.xml`, or `/usr/share/packages`), plus Vetro's own, at the lowest priority, in sequential calls of 30 packages so that the other root commands run in between, at most 220 packages, and no new call after about 50 seconds (`complete: false` says the result stopped early), and exports per package: whether a manifest was found, its SHA-256, the description (system and preinstalled packages and Vetro's own only), the declared privileges shortened to `tizen/<name>` or `samsung/<name>` (anything else is dropped, at most 40), and per component its kind, executable *file name*, the declared `on-boot`, `auto-restart` and `taskmanage`, the registry's `onboot` and `autorestart`, and `differs` (declared and registered flags disagree: the installer ignored what the package asked for, as with `vetrotvapp.svc` in issues #25 and #26; null when either is unknown). Further calls hash (SHA-256) the executables of components that start at boot: at most 40 files of at most 4 MB, 8 files and 8 MB per call; a failed call costs only its checksums. Timed on the Q80A (firmware T-NKM2DEUC-2310.0) with 220 packages, read-only, two runs: 2.8 to 3.2 s in total, the longest call 0.4 s, 218 of 220 manifests found, 10 checksums, a 143 KB section, and a trivial root command sent beside it waited 0.15 to 0.2 s (17 ms when idle). One TV, a fast one by the look of the other reports: slower TVs will take longer, and the 50 s limit and the batches are what keep that from mattering. Not exported: author, URLs, paths, the manifest text, metadata values, file contents. Without the switch the section reads `unavailable` with reason `not-requested`, and nothing is read. Not done: network connections per process, data folder sizes and activity (their commands are not verified on a TV and could cost time on a slow one).

Firmware and Tizen version: when the widget never posted its probe (reports #17 and #25 came back with an empty `device`), the service reads two lines itself, `Version=` from the `[Version]` section of `/etc/prd_info.ini` and the first line of `/etc/tizen-release`, and exports them as `firmware` (marked `firmwareSource: "service-read"`) and `tizenVersion`. A firmware the widget did post stays `firmwareSource: "widget-probe"` and is not replaced. Both values must match the shape the TVs write; anything else is dropped. The commercial model and the platform code are in neither file and still come only from the widget.

Tizen version without root: the widget also asks the TV for the public capability `http://tizen.org/feature/platform.version` (for example `6.5` or `9.0`) and posts it as `platformVersion`. That works in the app-only package too, where the root read above is not available. When both exist the capability wins. `tizenVersion` accepts two to four numeric parts; `tizenVersionSource` says where it came from: `widget-capability`, `service-read` or `unknown`. If no source can give a value, `tizenVersion` is `null` and the source is `unknown`; the report never fails because of it.

Model without the widget: when the widget posted no model, the service asks the TV's own remote-control API on `http://127.0.0.1:8001/api/v2/` (no root, 3 s limit) and keeps only `device.modelName`, if it looks like a model name. It is exported as `realModel` with `modelSource: "service-api"`; a model the widget posted is `modelSource: "widget-probe"` and is never replaced. The rest of that answer (ids, addresses, network names) is dropped before anything is stored.

How far the TV screen got: the Vetro screen sends a first report as soon as the service answers (step 6 of 9) and one after every later step, then the verdict report, then one more after the package list, which it reads last. `probe.screen` says how far the stored report got: `partial` (true if the screen was closed or stuck before its verdict), `done`, `of`, `next` (a step name from a fixed list), `elapsedSeconds`, `slow` (steps that ran out of time or were skipped; the widget's error text is not exported) and `carried` (`apps`/`packages` kept from the previous report because this one was sent before those steps ran). Reports from an older screen have `screen: null`.

Provenance: `device` and `probe` are values the Vetro widget posted to the service from the TV and the service stored; they are not read again during export. Both carry `source: "widget-probe"` and `probeAgeMinutes` (rounded minutes since the last post), so an old probe after a firmware update can be recognised. Only the TV itself may replace the stored probe. In `settings`, `access: "implemented"` means Vetro has a write path for the control; it does not mean the export wrote it or that the write was tested on this TV. Kernel threads are counted in `monitor.kernelThreadCount` instead of listed. Catalog `group` is null for menu entries with no parent.

Included: current authenticated root-agent connection state (not proof of persistence), platform/runtime status, model and firmware, package and component IDs/versions, classification, declared dependencies, disabled/boot/restart flags, current process names and resource metrics, numeric values of known TV settings (for each control, whether this TV exposes the method with the expected signature: `confirmed`, `missing` or `signature-mismatch`, plus the number of picture API methods it exposes), menu capability descriptors, public API probe availability, network connection methods, local filtering state, HbbTV/privacy state, launcher state (`launcher`: installed, on, running, whether Samsung’s Home is running, Fix TizenTube, whether Autorun Smart Hub was switched off, counts of tiles, skipped apps and failures, and whether a TizenTube tile exists; never the log, the error text or the app names), the quick wins (`quickWins`: auto update, MultiScreen autostart and Ambient as on, off, partial or unknown), the factory test setting (`factoryTest`: whether `db/FactoryApp/Aging` exists, its number and type, and whether it is on; read only), and MQTT connection counters. Catalog availability is not proof that a control works. Process data is a snapshot, not viewing history or a benchmark.

Network services in the `idle` state (visible or saved networks that are not connected) are not exported, so the report does not reveal how many networks are visible or saved.

Privacy policy `allowlist-v1` constructs a new object for every section. Unknown fields are dropped. Credentials, IP/MAC addresses, hostname, serial/boot/machine/MQTT identities, custom display names, author fields, SSIDs, configured DNS addresses, MQTT topics/payloads, process arguments/PIDs, URLs, raw error strings, user actions and consent records are excluded. Numeric metrics accept finite numbers only. Known technical string fields have bounded syntax and reject address/URL/user-path patterns.

Installed package IDs and current process names remain useful for analysis and can reveal which software is installed. This is a redacted technical report, not a guarantee against device fingerprinting. Arbitrary user-defined package IDs may themselves contain identifying text; review before sharing. There is no background collection or automatic sharing.

For analysis, check `schemaVersion`, each section status, nullable values, source limitations and incomplete dependencies before recommending changes. Null means unknown, not disabled. Do not infer write support from the firmware catalog. Preserve DNS configuration when changing filtering behavior.

The `platform.rooting` object identifies the current backend (`sdb-archive`) and its observed phase: `stopped`, `unsupported`, `preparing`, `sdb-handshake`, `launch-command`, `agent-callback`, `connected`, or `retrying`. Its nullable `failure` contains only a fixed `stage` and `code`, never command output, paths or secrets. An unsupported runtime stops at `unsupported` with `platformUnsupported`, preserving the Node 4 fallback without retries. Preparation failures distinguish `prepare-failed` from `token-write-failed`; SDB failures keep the transport's bounded codes (`sdbClosed`, `sdbReset`, `sdbTimeout`, and the other SDB error codes). `agent-timeout` means no authenticated callback arrived within the launch window, and `agent-disconnected` means the previously authenticated channel closed. A failure remains visible while retrying and clears when the agent authenticates or the service starts a new lifecycle. It is not a compatibility verdict: stream completion does not prove injection/archive execution succeeded, and `connected` does not prove cold-boot persistence. Packages without this instrumentation export null backend/phase.

The inventory's nullable `dependenciesAvailable` distinguishes an absent dependency table on older firmware (`false`) from a successful query (`true`) or unavailable information (`null`). When that table is absent, the main registry inventory and flags remain available, but each app's `dependencies` and `dependents` are null (Unknown), rather than empty lists (None declared). Other database errors and timeouts still fail the registry read.

Validation: privacy sentinel tests, failure-code, provenance and second-sample tests, partial failures and deadlines, concurrent HTTP downloads, download headers and browser JSON-file contents. A live download from the installed 0.2.1 integration on the Q80A returned all 14 sections (`release-installed.json`), and a community report from a Q67B on 0.2.3 returned 13 of 14. The `reason`, `sourceDirty`, provenance and two-sample changes have not yet been installed or exported on a TV. One download does not prove cold-boot persistence.
